# Shop visit: Riverside Cycle Works, 12 September

Everything on this page is invented. It ships with the kit so that the tools have something to chew on and so that a new reader can see the shape of a source entry: frontmatter at the top, the text kept whole, block anchors on the passages a wiki page will want to cite.

## The backlog

Sam Rivera keeps the repair backlog on a clipboard by the stand rather than in the booking software, because the software asks for a promised date at intake and Sam will not give one until the bike is on the stand and the problem is known. On the day of the visit the clipboard had nineteen tickets and the software had four. ^intake-backlog

Asked how long a ticket usually waits, Sam said it depends entirely on whether the part is in the drawer, and that the honest answer to a customer is "I will call you when I know", which is the answer the booking software has no field for.

## Pricing

Labour is charged in half-hour blocks and has not moved in two years. Parts are marked up at a flat rate that Sam described as "whatever keeps the lights on", and could not say where the number came from originally. The winter tune-up is priced as a package and is the only thing in the shop with a posted price. ^pricing-blocks

Sam's view is that a posted price is a promise about time as much as money, and that the reason the rest of the board is blank is not secrecy but that no two repairs take the same hour.

## Staffing

Two people, one of them part-time on weekends. A third was tried one summer and did not work out, for reasons Sam summarised as "the bench only fits two". ^bench-fits-two

## What was not said

Nobody put a number on what the backlog costs the shop in lost work, and the visit produced no basis for one. That is recorded as an open question on the project page rather than estimated here.
